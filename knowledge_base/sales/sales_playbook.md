## Key Capabilities (Internal Guidelines)

### BANT+ Qualification Framework (Mock)
- **Budget**: Does the customer have allocated budget for payment infrastructure? Are they comparing against an existing processor cost?
- **Authority**: Are you speaking with a decision-maker (VP Engineering, CTO, Head of Payments, CFO) or an influencer?
- **Need**: What specific pain points are driving the evaluation (developer experience, reliability, global expansion, cost, compliance)?
- **Timeline**: When do they plan to make a decision? Is there a triggering event (contract expiration, product launch, funding round)?
- **PLUS — Competition**: Who else are they evaluating? What's their current solution?
- **PLUS — Product Fit**: Which Stripe products map to their needs? Is this a single-product or multi-product opportunity?

### Discovery Best Practices (Mock Internal)
1. **Start with customer pain, not Stripe products**: "Tell me about your current payment setup and what's not working" before "Let me tell you about Stripe Checkout"
2. **Quantify the pain**: How many engineering hours do they spend on payments? What's their current downtime? What's their dispute rate?
3. **Multi-stakeholder mapping**: Identify the economic buyer (CFO, CEO), the technical champion (VP Eng, CTO), and the end user (developer, finance ops)
4. **Product fit first, pricing second**: Establish clear product requirements before discussing pricing. If pricing comes up early, acknowledge and defer: "I want to make sure we're the right fit before we talk numbers — can we come back to pricing once I understand your needs better?"
5. **Always run a technical validation**: For any deal > $100K annual volume, involve a Solutions Engineer for technical validation — never promise capabilities without SE confirmation
6. **Document everything in CRM**: Every customer interaction, product interest, competitor mention, and pricing discussion must be logged

### Deal Progression Milestones (Mock)
1. **Qualified Lead**: BANT+ criteria met, clear product fit identified
2. **Discovery Complete**: Full technical and business requirements documented
3. **Solution Proposed**: Written proposal with recommended products, implementation timeline, and indicative pricing
4. **Technical Validation**: SE confirms feasibility, identifies integration complexity
5. **Commercial Negotiation**: Pricing finalized, contract terms negotiated
6. **Closed Won / Lost**: Decision documented with win/loss reason

### Cross-Sell Triggers (Mock Internal)
- **Customer using Payments → discuss Radar** (fraud protection)
- **Customer using Payments + subscription model → discuss Billing**
- **Customer running a marketplace/platform → discuss Connect**
- **Customer with international customers → discuss Adaptive Pricing + Tax**
- **Customer asking about reporting → discuss Sigma or Data Pipeline**
- **Customer with finance team pain → discuss Revenue Recognition + Invoicing**

## Key Discovery Questions (Internal Reference — Use Discreetly)

- Has the customer been fully qualified against BANT+ criteria before investing significant sales resources?
- Is the decision-maker in the conversation, or are you one level removed?
- What is the compelling event driving their evaluation now vs. 6 months ago?
- Have you mapped all stakeholders and their individual priorities?
- Have you identified at least 2 potential cross-sell products based on their business model?
- Is there a competitive threat (incumbent contract renewal, RFP process)?

## Escalation Notes

- **Enterprise and Strategic deals require mandatory SE involvement before any technical commitments**
- **Multi-product deals involving Connect + other products require Connect specialist review**
- **If a deal stalls for > 30 days without clear reason, escalate to sales manager for deal review**
- **Competitive situations where Stripe is at a clear pricing disadvantage require deal desk escalation**
- **Any deal with regulatory/compliance complexity (fintech, gambling, crypto, adult) requires compliance pre-clearance before proceeding**
